## Query 4 (15:30)
- **Question:** For "memberships with at least one inbox item", which is faster — JOIN or EXISTS subquery?
- **Wiki hits:** none
- **Card hits:** none
- **Source:** research
- **Answer:** On modern Postgres/MySQL they're usually equivalent — the planner rewrites `IN (SELECT …)` / `EXISTS` into a semi-join. `EXISTS` can short-circuit per row; JOIN requires DISTINCT which sorts/hashes the result. Prefer EXISTS/IN for existence checks.

## Query 5 (20:28)

- **Question:** What is better for performance in SQL when checking an association exists (e.g. memberships with inbox items) — JOIN or subquery?
- **Wiki hits:** none (top results [[rails/delegated-type]], [[sql/sql-index]] not relevant)
- **Card hits:** none
- **Web check:** confirms — Postgres planner rewrites `IN (SELECT …)`, `=ANY`, and `EXISTS` to the same Semi-Join plan; EXISTS short-circuits on first match; INNER JOIN requires DISTINCT to dedupe (sort/hash cost). MySQL 8 transforms IN/EXISTS to semijoin with strategies (FirstMatch, LooseScan, Materialize, Duplicate Weedout); 8.0.16+ decorrelates trivially correlated EXISTS to be optimized like IN. NOT IN is the notable exception — doesn't convert to anti-join, unlike NOT EXISTS / LEFT JOIN IS NULL. URLs: https://www.percona.com/blog/sql-optimizations-in-postgresql-in-vs-exists-vs-any-all-vs-join/ and https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/semijoins-antijoins.html
- **Source:** research
- **Answer:** Usually equivalent on modern Postgres/MySQL — planner rewrites to semi-join. Prefer EXISTS (short-circuits, no DISTINCT needed); INNER JOIN + DISTINCT works but adds dedup cost; NOT IN is the one to avoid for negation (use NOT EXISTS). Real lever is indexing the FK column.
